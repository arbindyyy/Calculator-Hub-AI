The 30% Rule for Rent

A widely used guideline for housing is the 30% rule, which suggests that you should spend no more than 30% of your gross (pre-tax) monthly income on housing costs. This includes rent and, in some interpretations, basic utilities.

This calculator uses the 30% rule to give you a baseline for what might be an affordable rent. Exceeding this percentage could strain your budget and make it difficult to cover other expenses and save for your financial goals.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is the 30% rule always accurate?

The 30% rule is a general guideline and may not be perfect for everyone. In high-cost-of-living areas, it might be necessary to spend more. Conversely, if you have significant debt, you might want to spend less. It's a starting point, and you should adjust it based on your personal budget and financial situation.

What other costs should I budget for besides rent?

When renting, you should also budget for utilities (electricity, gas, water, internet), renter's insurance, moving costs, and potential application fees or security deposits. These costs are not included in this calculator's estimate.
