Understanding Loan Amortization

Amortization refers to the process of paying off a debt over time in regular installments. For a loan, each payment you make consists of two parts: principal and interest. At the beginning of the loan, a larger portion of your payment goes toward interest. As you continue to make payments, more of each payment goes toward reducing the principal balance.

This calculator generates a detailed amortization schedule, which is a table that shows a breakdown of each payment you will make over the life of the loan. It helps you visualize how your debt is being paid down.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is the difference between principal and interest?

Principal is the amount of money you originally borrowed from the lender. Interest is the cost of borrowing that money, essentially a fee paid to the lender. Your goal is to pay back the principal, and your payments include interest as the lender's profit.

Why does the interest portion of my payment decrease over time?

Interest is calculated based on your outstanding loan balance. As you make payments and reduce your principal balance, the amount of interest charged each month also decreases. Since your total monthly payment is fixed, a smaller interest portion means a larger principal portion, which accelerates your debt repayment over time.
