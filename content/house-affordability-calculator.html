How Home Affordability is Calculated

This calculator estimates an affordable home price based on the debt-to-income (DTI) ratio, a standard metric used by lenders. A common guideline is the 28/36 rule. The "28" refers to your housing expenses (mortgage, taxes, insurance), which should ideally be no more than 28% of your gross monthly income. The "36" refers to your total debt, which should be no more than 36% of your gross monthly income. This calculator uses the 36% back-end ratio for a more comprehensive estimate.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is a debt-to-income (DTI) ratio?

The DTI ratio compares how much you owe each month to how much you earn. Specifically, it's the percentage of your gross monthly income (before taxes) that goes towards paying your monthly debt payments (e.g., mortgage, car loans, student loans, credit card payments). Lenders use it to assess your ability to manage monthly payments and repay debts.

How can I increase my home affordability?

You can increase the amount of home you can afford by increasing your income, paying down existing debts to lower your DTI ratio, or increasing your down payment. A larger down payment reduces the required loan amount, which can help you qualify for a more expensive home.
