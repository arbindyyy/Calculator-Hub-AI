What is Debt-to-Income (DTI) Ratio?

Your debt-to-income (DTI) ratio is a key financial metric that compares your total monthly debt payments to your gross monthly income. This percentage is one of the main factors lenders use to assess your ability to manage monthly payments and repay a new loan. A lower DTI ratio indicates a healthy balance between debt and income, while a higher ratio can signal that you have too much debt for your income level.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What's a good DTI ratio?

Lenders generally prefer a DTI ratio of 36% or less. While you might be able to get a loan with a DTI up to 43%, a ratio above this is often seen as risky, making it harder to qualify for new credit.

How can I lower my DTI ratio?

There are two main ways to lower your DTI: increase your income or decrease your debt. You can lower your debt by paying off loans, reducing credit card balances, or avoiding new debt. Increasing your income through a raise, side hustle, or other means will also lower your DTI.
